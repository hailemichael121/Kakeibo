The story

Before the spreadsheet, there was a notebook.

A short history of a household habit, and how this app keeps it.

1What it means
家計簿

A household account book.

Kakeibo (家計簿) means “household account book”. It is a notebook in which a home’s income and spending are written down by hand, and then looked at again.

The idea is plain: money is easier to understand once it is visible.

21904

Hani Motoko and the early 1900s.

Kakeibo was popularized in Japan in 1904 by the journalist Hani Motoko. Her aim, as it is usually described, was to help ordinary households keep clear records of their money.

This account stays deliberately modest. Details beyond that are best read in a good history of the period, and this page does not put words in her mouth.

3The household ledger

Writing it down, then looking back.

The practice rests on two habits: recording what comes in and goes out, and pausing regularly to read the record. The writing makes spending visible. The pause makes it useful.

Make the money visible, then decide what matters.
4The principles

Record. Notice. Adjust.

Record honestly, without trying to be perfect. Notice where money gathered. Choose one small thing to change next time.

The four categories (Survival, Optional, Culture and Extra) are a lens for understanding a purchase. They are not a score, and they are not moral labels.

5How it evolved

From notebook to modern budgeting.

The modern Kakeibo method is not identical to what households did in 1904. It is a later interpretation that keeps the spirit of the original and adds a structure of monthly planning and reflection.

6Why it still matters

Attention is the point.

Most of us have more ways to track money than ever, and less time to look at it. A slow, regular habit asks for a little attention, and returns a clearer picture of what you value.

It can suit people who like journaling and gentle routines. It does take regular entry, and missed purchases make a review less complete. Capture what you can, then return without blame.

7In this app

The same practice, kept on your device.

Kakeibo, the app, follows a four-step monthly cycle. Start: record income and fixed costs, with a savings target if you want one. Track: log purchases and take a one-tap pause. End: look over the month’s totals. Reflect: choose one thing to keep, change or explore.

The native apps are built to work offline, with no account, and to keep your records close to you.
